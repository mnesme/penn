The process

How a pen gets made

Six steps. Your first one takes an afternoon. By the tenth, you'll have it down to an hour.

1

Choose your blank — it's where the story starts

A "blank" is a small block of wood that becomes the body of your pen. Hardwoods like walnut and cherry are easiest for beginners. But here's what experienced turners know: the blank is what makes a pen truly meaningful. Wood from a fallen tree in your yard, a piece of reclaimed flooring, a branch from a grandparent's garden — the material carries the story. Your first pen doesn't need to be exotic, but keep that in mind as you grow.

Wood pen blanks → Acrylic & exotic blanks
2

Cut & drill your blank

Cut the blank to length and drill a precise hole through the centre to fit the brass tube from your pen kit. Accuracy here matters for the final fit — the PenPal lathe includes a drilling guide to make this foolproof.

Pen blank drilling chuck →
3

Glue in the brass tube

Lightly roughen the brass tube with sandpaper so the glue bites, then apply CA glue (super glue) and slide it into the drilled blank. Let it set for a few minutes. This tube is what the pen mechanism presses into during final assembly.

CA glue → Tube insertion tool
4

Square the ends & mount on the lathe

Use the barrel trimmer to square off both ends of the blank flush with the brass tube — this is what makes the metal parts fit cleanly. Then mount the blank on the lathe using the pen mandrel.

Barrel trimmer → Pen mandrel →
5

Turn & sand the barrel

This is the most satisfying part — and where most first-timers make their mistakes. You might go too deep, catch an edge, or sand through a thin section. That's completely normal. Every turner did the same. The blank is cheap; the lesson is valuable. Work the wood down to match the diameter of the bushings, then sand through grits (120 → 220 → 400 → 600) until it's glass-smooth.

Turning chisels → Sandpaper →
6

Finish & assemble

Apply your finish while the blank is still spinning on the lathe — CA finish gives a glassy look in minutes. Once dry, use the pen press to push the metal components in firmly and squarely. Done. That's your pen.

CA finish → Pen press → Pen kits →

Common questions

Things beginners worry about

None at all. Pen turning is actually how most people get into woodworking — not the other way around. The pieces are small, the lathe does most of the work, and the process is forgiving. Your first pen won't be perfect, but it will be a usable, beautiful pen. Most beginners are genuinely surprised at how good it looks.
Very little. The PenPal lathe sits on a workbench or kitchen table. You need roughly 2–3 feet of clear worktop, plus somewhere to stand. Many people start in a spare bedroom or garage corner. The main consideration is dust — work near an open window or invest in a small shop vac.
The Bolt Action pen is the most popular choice for beginners — it's simple, satisfying to make, and looks great. The Slimline is the cheapest kit if budget is the priority, but some find it slightly trickier due to its thinner profile. We include bolt action kits in our starter sets for exactly this reason.
The PenPal is purpose-designed for pen turning — it excels at exactly this task. If you later want to turn larger items like bottle openers, bowls or candle holders, the 10" Midi Lathe is the upgrade path. But most dedicated pen turners stick with the PenPal indefinitely; it does what it does extremely well.
